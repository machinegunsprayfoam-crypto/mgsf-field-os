⚠ THE MONTANA RULE — READ FIRST

The deadliest insulation in America came out of our state. The W.R. Grace vermiculite mine in Libby, Montana supplied most of the "Zonolite" attic insulation sold in the U.S. from the 1920s to 1990 — and it was contaminated with asbestos. Hundreds of Libby residents died; the EPA declared a public health emergency there. Montana homes got more of this product than almost anywhere. Crew rule: any attic with loose pebble-like fill gets treated as Zonolite until proven otherwise. Nobody touches it, sweeps it, or sprays over it. Job stops, customer gets told, testing/abatement gets quoted. This rule is not negotiable and it is also a trust-builder — the contractor who protects the family from the attic is the contractor who gets the whole house.

PART 1 — The old stuff you'll open a wall and find

Vermiculite / Zonolite RED — STOP WORK

Looks like: loose pebbles the size of popcorn kernels or smaller — gray-gold, silvery-brown; each piece has accordion-like layers and often a slight metallic glitter. Poured between joists, sometimes under newer insulation. 📷 EPA photo guide
R-value: 2.1–2.4/inch — poor, which is why attics with it are usually cold AND dangerous.
Handling: DON'T. EPA guidance: assume it contains Libby asbestos; do not disturb, do not sweep, do not store boxes on it, do not blow insulation over it, do not let cable guys crawl it. If a bid requires touching it: licensed asbestos abatement contractor, state asbestos-program notification, negative-air containment — not our trade, we refer and re-quote after clearance.
Disposal: abatement contractor's problem — wetted, double-bagged 6-mil, manifested to an approved asbestos-accepting landfill. Never in a normal dumpster.
Lifespan: the mineral lasts forever; the hazard does too.
MGSF play: attic inspections that CATCH Zonolite are a service. "Test before you touch" builds the trust that closes the whole-house job after clearance.

Asbestos lagging, block & board RED — STOP WORK

Looks like: white/gray plaster-like "mud" wrapped around old boiler pipes, corrugated-paper pipe wrap (looks like cardboard), rigid chalky boards, gray-white cement sheets; also in old vinyl floor tile (9"×9") and transite siding. Common in pre-1980 basements, schools, farmhouses with gravity furnaces.
R-value: ~2–3/inch (irrelevant — nobody keeps it for performance).
Handling / disposal: same as Zonolite — licensed abatement only, state program rules, manifested disposal. Friable (crumbly) asbestos is the most dangerous kind and old lagging is almost always friable.
Lifespan: indefinite; gets MORE dangerous as it ages and crumbles.

UFFI — urea-formaldehyde foam AMBER

Looks like: dried shaving cream — butterscotch to pale yellow, dull, crumbles to powder under a finger. Injected into wall cavities in the late 1970s energy crisis; look for patched injection holes outside.
R-value: ~4–5/inch when new; it shrinks up to 3% linear as it cures/ages, opening gaps — real-world walls perform far worse.
Handling: the formaldehyde scare is mostly historical — off-gassing was strongest in the first months and these installs are 45+ years old. The real hazard now is fine dust: P100 respirator, suits, wet-mist before removal.
Disposal: bag it; ordinary C&D landfill accepts it. Not classed hazardous once cured/aged.
Lifespan: effectively over — shrunken, crumbly, and a removal/replace candidate wherever found. Prime retrofit lead for injection foam or drill-and-fill.

Sawdust, shavings, newspaper, corncobs, balsam wool, old rock wool GREEN

Looks like: exactly what they are. Balsam wool = brown wood-fiber batts in black kraft paper sleeves (often printed "Balsam-Wool"). Old rock wool = dull gray wooly pellets or matted fluff, heavier than fiberglass.
R-value: sawdust/paper ~2–2.5; rock wool 3.0–3.3; balsam ~2.4. All usually settled, gapped, and rodent-mined by now.
Handling: standard PPE — but old attics mean rodent droppings (hantavirus country out here): P100, gloves, don't dry-sweep, mist first. One check: loose gray-gold pebbles mixed in = possible vermiculite blend → RED rule applies.
Disposal: C&D landfill; call the county transfer station ahead for big vac-out volumes.
Lifespan: long past done. Every one of these is a removal + air-seal + foam/cellulose upgrade story.

PART 2 — The current workhorses (what we compete against)

Fiberglass — batts and blown GREEN

Looks like: pink/yellow/white/green cotton-candy batts (paper or foil faced) or fluffy loose fill.
R-value: batts 2.9–3.8/inch (R-13 to R-15 in a 2×4 wall); blown attic fill 2.2–2.7/inch. Real-world performance drops hard with any air movement — it filters air, it doesn't stop it. That's our #1 honest sales point: foam insulates AND air-seals.
Handling: glass-fiber itch and lung irritation — N95 minimum (P100 in blown attics), long sleeves, goggles. No exotic hazards.
Disposal: C&D landfill. No special rules.
Lifespan: 25–50+ years, but sag, wind-washing, moisture, and critters usually degrade it decades sooner. Wet fiberglass never recovers — replace.

Cellulose — blown and dense-pack GREEN

Looks like: gray shredded newspaper. Dense-pack in walls, loose in attics.
R-value: 3.2–3.8/inch. Loose attic fill settles 15–20% over years — R on day one isn't R in year ten.
Handling: very dusty — P100 in enclosed spaces; treated with borate fire retardants (mild irritant, not exotic).
Disposal: C&D landfill; compacts well in vac bags.
Lifespan: 20–30 years useful; moisture events ruin it (holds water, mats, grows mold).

Mineral wool (modern Rockwool), cotton/denim, sheep's wool GREEN

Looks like: mineral wool = dense gray-green batts/boards, feels like stiff felt; denim = blue batts; wool = obvious.
R-value: mineral wool 3.7–4.3/inch (and genuinely fireproof + water-shedding); denim 3.4–3.7; wool 3.5–3.8.
Handling: standard PPE; mineral wool fibers itch like fiberglass.
Disposal: C&D landfill; wool/denim compostable in principle.
Lifespan: mineral wool 50+; naturals 30–50 if dry and pest-treated.

Rigid foam boards — EPS, XPS, Polyiso GREEN

Looks like: EPS = white beadboard (coffee-cup foam); graphite EPS = gray; XPS = smooth pink/blue/green boards; polyiso = yellowish foam with foil or fiber facers.
R-value: EPS 3.6–4.2/inch · XPS 5.0 new, drifts to ~4.5–4.7 as blowing gas escapes over decades · polyiso 5.6–6.5 rated, BUT derates in cold — at Montana winter temps polyiso can drop to ~R-5/inch or below. Zone 6/7 design rule: don't count polyiso's label R for winter-critical exterior layers; XPS/EPS hold steadier when cold.
Handling: standard PPE; cutting = light dust; all foam boards are combustible — code requires thermal/ignition barriers same as our spray foam (IRC R316).
Disposal: C&D landfill; clean EPS can go to densifier recycling where it exists.
Lifespan: 50+ years physically.

PART 3 — Our products (the full safety story)

Spray polyurethane foam — open-cell & closed-cell AMBER while spraying GREEN once cured

Looks like: open-cell = soft, spongy, off-white/yellow, dents with a thumb; closed-cell = rigid, dense, cream-to-amber, won't dent.
R-value: open-cell 3.5–3.8/inch · closed-cell 6.0–7.0/inch (modern HFO-blown at the top of that range). Closed-cell is also a Class II vapor retarder at ~1.5–2" and adds racking strength.
Handling — application (the AMBER part): the A-side is isocyanate (MDI) — respiratory sensitizer. Full protocol: supplied-air respirator or PAPR with organic vapor/P100 for the sprayer and helper in the spray zone, chemical suits, gloves, eye pro; mechanical ventilation of the work zone; occupants and pets OUT; re-entry/re-occupancy per manufacturer (commonly 24 hours, verify the system's TDS); no ignition sources during and right after spraying; hot-work ban near fresh foam. This is exactly what the free CPI Chemical Health & Safety cert drills — every crew member carries it.
Handling — cured: inert and safe. Cutting/grinding/removal of cured foam = nuisance dust only: P100, eye pro. Fire: cured foam burns — thermal barrier (½" drywall) or ignition barrier per code in living/crawl/attic spaces.
Disposal: cured foam scraps = ordinary C&D landfill. Drums are the real rule: empty A-side (ISO) drums get decontaminated/neutralized per supplier instructions, bungs OUT (never seal — pressure), supplier take-back or licensed drum recycler; B-side (resin) drums rinse and recycle. Never send a wet ISO drum to a landfill.
Lifespan: life of the structure — 80+ years. Doesn't settle, doesn't sag, doesn't feed rodents. Closed-cell survives incidental wetting; open-cell must be kept dry (roof leaks show through it fast — which is honestly a leak-detection feature).

Injection foam (wall-cavity tripolymer) AMBER

What/looks like: shaving-cream-consistency foam injected into closed walls through holes; cures soft, white. The modern, stable successor to UFFI's niche.
R-value: 4.6–5.0/inch.
Safety/disposal/lifespan: respirator + ventilation during injection; cured = inert, C&D disposal; 50+ years, minimal shrink in modern formulas. A natural drill-and-fill service line for the thousands of empty-wall farmhouses in our territory.

PART 4 — What's coming (watch list)

The near-future shelf GREEN

Aerogel blankets: ~R-10/inch — the highest practical R per inch sold today. Costly; used where space is worth money (thin retrofits, rim joists in historic buildings). Very dusty to cut — P100. Watch for price drops.
Vacuum insulated panels: R-25–40/inch until punctured — one screw kills a panel dead. Appliances today, niche building panels tomorrow; 20–30 yr vacuum life. Not a rural-retrofit product yet.
Wood-fiber batts/boards: R-3.4–4.0/inch, U.S. production ramping (TimberHP, Maine). Vapor-open, carbon-storing — the green-build spec darling. Compostable disposal.
Hemp batts / hempcrete: R-3.5–3.7 batts; hempcrete ~R-2/inch but massive + moisture-buffering. Montana grows hemp — keep an eye on local supply.
Graphite EPS (gray board): R-4.7–5.0/inch at EPS pricing — quietly replacing white EPS in exterior sheathing jobs.
Mycelium panels: grown fungal insulation, experimental, R~3–4. Years out.
Already-here future in OUR rigs: HFO-blown closed-cell (low global-warming blowing agents, top-end R ~7/inch) and bio-based (soy-polyol) blends — spec sheets to watch each reorder.

PART 5 — Working across state lines (MT · ND · SD · WY)

Same physics, different paperwork. Zonolite shipped to all four states, so the Montana Rule travels with the truck. What changes at the border is licensing and who runs the asbestos program. Verify contacts before the first job in each state — programs update.

Crew rules — one screen

1. Loose pebbly gray-gold fill = STOP, assume Zonolite, tell Clifton. 2. Pre-1980 boiler/pipe wrap = STOP, assume asbestos. 3. Every removal: P100, gloves, suits, mist before disturbing, never dry-sweep (dust + hantavirus). 4. Spraying: supplied air/PAPR, ventilate, occupants out, re-entry per TDS, no flame. 5. Drums: bungs out, neutralize ISO, supplier take-back. 6. Cured foam, fiberglass, cellulose, boards → C&D landfill; call the transfer station before hauling a big vac-out. 7. Crossing a state line: MT = roll · ND = state license first · SD = excise license + 2% in the bid · WY = ask the city. 8. When in doubt: photo → send to Clifton/Klyfton → wait.
